# Ethics statement — TextGuard

## Dual use

This project builds **attacks** on forecasters that read text: edits that reverse a genuine
report's direction (P3 flip), flip it only where a defence will not notice (R3 A1), append a
forward-looking lie (A2), or overstate a change (×3, ×10). An attacker could use the same ideas to
manipulate a deployed forecaster, for example one that reads market news. We judge the release
responsible for the reasons below, and we release less than we could.

## Why releasing is responsible

1. **The attacks are deliberately weak and visible.** Every fake is a **minimal, rule-based edit of
   a genuine report**: a few direction words and the numbers that depend on them. Nothing is
   hidden, no homoglyphs or invisible text, and no LLM-written fluent fakes (R5 was not run). A human
   reading the text sees the claim. Rules were chosen for auditability and to avoid same-model
   circularity, not for attack strength. A real adversary can already do more than this with any
   chatbot.
2. **The idea is not new; the measurement and the defence are.** Headline attacks on price
   forecasters are already published (Rizvani et al., 2026, imperceptible attacks; *Market Signal
   Injection*, 2026). Our contribution is a metric (DSS) and a training-free defence that deployers
   can run on their own models. The toolkit is an **audit**: it reports how far lies move *your*
   model and what each defence costs.
3. **Defence-oriented release.** The public API (`textguard.audit`, `TextGuardV1/V2`) measures and
   defends. Its only built-in attack is the one-sentence A2 (domain-free wording), which a deployer
   needs in order to test the failure we found. Domain-specific attack generation is not offered
   for new text styles. Users bring their own test fakes.
4. **Honest limits are part of the release.** The model card lists every known failure (A2 evades
   v1; v2 blocks 22% of genuine headline bundles; no transfer to Qwen2.5-7B), so nobody deploys the
   defence believing it is stronger than it is.

## Labelling

Every generated attack text carries `synthetic`, `attacked`, `real_direction` and
`claimed_direction` columns, and `texts/r4/README.txt` states that synthetic rows are fake. The
historical R4 input file in git history has no `synthetic` column. Its fakes are the forecast rows
with `attacked = True` and the judge rows whose `sources` lack `real` (`DATA_CARD.md` §1a). The
toolkit's audit outputs name the attack (`a2`, `user`) on every row. Fake texts must not be
published or fed to any production system outside a controlled test.

## Data and people

The data are public statistical reports (U.S. government series summarised by Time-MMD) and public
financial news headlines (FNSPID / Benzinga) about listed companies. No personal data is used. No
human-subjects study was run (R5's plausibility study was not run). The labels were written by the
author.
